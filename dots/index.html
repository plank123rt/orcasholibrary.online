A dark sky, considered closely

Pick any direction.

And follow it outward.

In a universe with no edge, that line can only end one way —

on the surface of a star.

Shell after shell
2× distance ¼ light
same shell 4× stars

They cancel exactly. Every shell, forever.

So the night sky should not be dark at all.

It should burn as bright as the sun — everywhere you look.

Olbers' paradox — 1823

It doesn't.

The universe hasn't existed long enough for that light to arrive.

Most of that starlight is still on its way —

crossing space, right now, toward you.

And what does arrive has been stretched by an expanding universe —

pulled past red, past what your eyes can catch.

The dark above you tonight isn't empty.

It's light — still on its way.
