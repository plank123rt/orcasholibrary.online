01 · Mechanism

Why electrolyte imbalance causes fatigue

Sodium (Na⁺), potassium (K⁺), and chloride (Cl⁻) are the ions that keep cells electrically and osmotically stable. Every nerve signal and muscle contraction in the body depends on a gradient of these ions across the cell membrane, and that gradient is maintained by a single, continuously running pump.

Na⁺ / K⁺ – ATPase pump, one cycle
3 Na⁺ (in) + 2 K⁺ (out) + ATP + H₂O → 3 Na⁺ (out) + 2 K⁺ (in) + ADP + Pᵢ

Each cycle spends one molecule of ATP to move three sodium ions out of the cell and two potassium ions in. The deficit this creates is what holds the resting membrane potential steady.

When sweating or low intake depletes these ions, the pump runs short of substrate, and the effect moves through the system in sequence:

Electrolyte depletion

Sweating, heat, or low fluid and salt intake reduces Na⁺, K⁺ and Cl⁻ available to the cell.

01

Membrane potential drifts

Fewer ions to move means the voltage difference the pump maintains across the membrane becomes harder to hold steady.

02

ATP generation is impaired

Cellular energy production is itself sensitive to the ionic environment it operates in, so output falls alongside the imbalance.

03

Neuromuscular signalling slows

Nerve firing and muscle contraction both rely on the same ion gradients, so signalling efficiency drops.

Fatigue, weakness, low energy

Together, this is what is felt on a hot day or after heavy exertion.

02 · Water Alone Isn't Enough

Why plain water doesn't restore the balance

Water moves into and out of cells by osmosis, following the concentration of dissolved ions rather than the other way around. Drinking water without ions to go with it does not resupply the gradient the pump depends on.

Intake vs. restoration
H₂O (intake) ≠ Na⁺ / K⁺ / Cl⁻ restoration

Without ions, water dilutes what's already in circulation rather than replenishing the intracellular gradient, which is why plain water can leave a person still feeling drained even after drinking a good amount.

03 · Daily Use

Recommended preparation

A simple routine to sip through the day, in place of plain water.

  1. Start with ~500 ml of normal drinking water.
  2. Add 1 sachet of electrolyte mix.
  3. Add 2 drops of H4O.
  4. Mix and sip through the day, the way you would drink normal water.

General recommendation

This routine is suitable for most people, including family members and friends, particularly during summer or periods of fatigue. It supports hydration, electrolyte balance, and general energy stability as part of a normal daily routine.
