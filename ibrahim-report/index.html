🧬 Primary Diagnosis: Niemann-Pick Disease, Type C1 (NPC1)

Confirmed by Whole Exome Sequencing (Redcliffe Labs, reported 18 Nov 2022): a homozygous, likely-pathogenic frameshift variant in NPC1, c.336_337delAT (p.Cys113Ter), exon 4, autosomal recessive inheritance. This is consistent with the parents' consanguineous marriage. The variant is novel (not previously reported in gnomAD or 1kG databases) and causes premature protein truncation.

🔬 What NPC1 normally does

The NPC1 gene encodes a large protein sitting in the membrane of lysosomes (the cell's recycling compartments). Its job is to shuttle cholesterol out of the lysosome after LDL particles are broken down inside it, handing the cholesterol off from a partner protein (NPC2) and passing it on to the rest of the cell.[1,2] When NPC1 is truncated, as in this child's variant, cholesterol and related fatty lipids become trapped inside lysosomes instead of being redistributed. Over time this traps macrophages, liver cells, spleen cells and neurons in an overloaded state, which is why NPC1 disease affects the spleen, liver and brain together rather than just one organ.

🔬 Why a cherry-red spot appears

The macula (the eye's central retina) is thin except for the fovea, which lacks the layer of ganglion-cell neurons that covers the rest of the retina. In lysosomal storage diseases, lipid builds up inside those surrounding ganglion cells and makes them look pale and opaque. The thin, ganglion-cell-free fovea in the middle keeps its normal reddish colour because the underlying choroidal blood vessels still show through, so it appears as a "cherry-red" dot surrounded by a pale halo.[3] This sign appears in Niemann-Pick disease as well as in other sphingolipid storage disorders such as Tay-Sachs disease, so it is a useful pointer toward lysosomal storage disease but not proof of any single diagnosis on its own.

🔬 What chitotriosidase measures

Chitotriosidase is an enzyme released in large amounts by activated macrophages, the immune cells that end up stuffed with undigested lipid in storage disorders. Because it is a marker of macrophage activation rather than of any single disease, it rises across several lysosomal storage diseases, including Gaucher disease and the Niemann-Pick family, not just NPC.[4,5] The value here (905.81, against a cutoff below 150) points strongly toward macrophage overload. Combined with normal sphingomyelinase (which rules out Niemann-Pick A/B) and normal beta-glucosidase (which rules out Gaucher disease), the pattern narrows the differential specifically toward NPC, which is exactly the logic the genetics note followed.

Peripheral smear impression: Microcytic hypochromic anemia with mild erythrocytosis; anisopoikilocytosis, target cells, elliptocytes.

The Mentzer index (MCV divided by RBC count) is a quick bedside tool to separate iron deficiency from thalassemia trait when red cells are small. Here that works out to roughly 60.1 ÷ 5.81 ≈ 10.3. Values under 13 generally favour a thalassemia trait pattern (small red cells the bone marrow overproduces to compensate) over simple iron deficiency (which tends to give a normal or high Mentzer index because the total red cell count usually falls, not rises). 🔬 Reading the acid-base picture

Both samples show low pCO₂ with a high-normal to elevated pH, a respiratory alkalosis pattern. This happens when fast breathing (tachypnea) driven by fever or respiratory distress blows off more carbon dioxide than usual, shifting blood pH upward. It is a common, usually self-limiting finding during febrile respiratory illness in young children and does not by itself indicate a metabolic or NPC-related problem. The drift toward higher sodium and lower potassium by 6 Jan is worth flagging to the treating team, since it can reflect dehydration or the effects of IV fluids and diuresis during the admission rather than the underlying storage disease.

    💊 How Miglustat works

    Miglustat is a small oral molecule that blocks glucosylceramide synthase, the enzyme that carries out the first committed step in building glycosphingolipids (a family of fatty molecules). By slowing their production, it reduces the amount of lipid that has to be cleared by the already overwhelmed lysosomal system, rather than fixing the underlying transport defect directly. Because it can cross the blood-brain barrier, it is used specifically to help slow the progressive neurological component of NPC, and it is the only substrate reduction therapy specifically authorized for NPC in several jurisdictions.[6,7] It does not reverse existing damage and works best as a long-term, continuously taken therapy, so confirming whether it was actually started and is being taken consistently is clinically important.
