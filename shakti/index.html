A CYCLE-FRIENDLY MOVEMENT PLAN, MADE FOR YOU

Welcome, Shakti

Fifteen minutes, six months, one steadier you. Gentle, doable movement built around your actual school day, made to support your cycle rather than fight it.

WHY THESE MOVES, NOT A WORKOUT

What we're actually doing here

Blood flow to the pelvis

Slow hip openers and gentle flexion increase circulation to the pelvic muscles and lower back, which is where a lot of period discomfort and heaviness of flow originates.

Calming the stress hormone

Cortisol competes with the hormones that regulate your cycle. Slow breathing and restorative poses turn that down, which is often more useful than a hard cardio session.

Light strength, not more burn

Since a lean body doesn't need to lose weight to help its cycle, we use light resistance to build muscle signalling without adding to an already tight energy budget.

WHEN SHE'LL ACTUALLY DO THIS

6:45 – 7:00 AM

THE SIX MONTH ARC

Three slow phases, two months each

Months 1–2Months 3–4Months 5–6
Foundation
Months 1–2 · settle the nervous system

Goal: teach the body that it's safe, through breath and slow mobility. Nothing here should ever feel like effort.

4-7-8 breathing
3 minutes
Sit or lie down. Breathe in through the nose for 4 counts, hold for 7, breathe out slowly through the mouth for 8. Repeat.
Lowers cortisol before it can compete with your cycle hormones.
Cat-cow flow
2 minutes, slow
On hands and knees, arch the back up on the exhale, dip it down on the inhale. Move with the breath, not a count.
Mobilises the lower spine and pelvis where cramping and heaviness sit.
Butterfly stretch
2 minutes
Sit, soles of the feet together, knees relaxed toward the floor. Sit tall, no bouncing, just breathe into it.
Opens the hips and increases circulation to the pelvic floor.
Child's pose with sway
2 minutes
Kneel, hips to heels, arms stretched forward, forehead down. Gently sway the hips side to side.
A full nervous-system reset, deeply restful for the lower back.
Legs up the wall
3 to 4 minutes
Lie on your back, legs resting up a wall or the side of a bed, arms open. Just breathe.
A classic for calming the whole system, especially good in the evening.
Build
Months 3–4 · gentle strength

Goal: light bodyweight strength. Muscle helps hormone signalling even without any weight change, so this stays easy on purpose.

Bodyweight squats
2 sets of 8 to 10
Feet hip-width, sit back like reaching for a chair, chest lifted. Slow down, slow up.
Builds the biggest muscles in the body with zero impact.
Glute bridges
2 sets of 10
Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat. Lift the hips up, squeeze, lower slowly.
Strengthens the muscles that support the pelvis and lower back.
Wall push-ups
2 sets of 8
Hands on a wall, shoulder height. Bend the elbows to bring the chest toward the wall, push back.
Builds upper body strength gently, no floor needed.
Bird-dog
2 sets of 8 each side
On hands and knees, extend the opposite arm and leg straight out, hold 2 seconds, return.
Trains balance and core control without any strain.
Clamshells
2 sets of 10 each side
Lie on your side, knees bent, feet together. Open the top knee like a clamshell, close slowly.
Strengthens the hips, which steadies the pelvis during activity.
Flow
Months 5–6 · confidence and consistency

Goal: everything from the last two phases, now flowing together, plus short easy walks. Still capped at 15 minutes.

Sun salutation, slow version
3 rounds
Reach up, fold forward, step back to a gentle plank, lower, cobra, downward dog, step forward, rise.
Links breath, strength and stretch into one flowing sequence.
Tree pose
1 minute each side
Stand tall, one foot resting on the inner calf or thigh, hands together at the chest. Find a still point to look at.
Balance work quietly builds core and ankle strength.
Easy walk
8 to 10 minutes, relaxed pace
Around the block or a terrace, phone away, just noticing surroundings. Not a power walk.
Movement without pushing the energy budget too hard.
Band rows
2 sets of 10
Loop a band around a door handle, pull the elbows straight back, squeeze the shoulder blades, release slowly.
Keeps building upper body and posture strength.
Gratitude breath cooldown
2 minutes
Sit quietly, hand on the belly, breathe slowly, and name one thing that went well today.
Closes the session calmly and builds the habit of noticing progress.

FOR THE ADULTS IN THE ROOM

Where this is grounded

This plan favours mobility, breath and light resistance over aerobic or fat-loss style exercise, since that approach fits a lean, low-flow presentation better than a generic PCOS program built for weight loss.

  • International Evidence-based Guideline for the Assessment and Management of Polycystic Ovary Syndrome, 2023
  • Research on the lean PCOS phenotype and the role of resistance exercise and micronutrient support
  • Adolescent lean PCOS studies on insulin resistance independent of body weight
  • Clinical guidance on functional hypothalamic amenorrhea and energy availability in adolescents
  • ACOG patient guidance on menstrual health in adolescents
One small, steady thing a day. Fifteen minutes doesn't ask much of a school day, and that's the whole point.
