01 · The Climate Factor

Humid air stresses your skin barrier

In places like Chennai, warmth + moisture stay high almost all year - a perfect setup for fungal growth, trapped sweat, and barrier breakdown.

70–90% humid-city RH
🦠Fungal growth
💧Trapped sweat
🛡️Weaker barrier

Skin stays healthiest around 40–60% humidity. Far outside that range - too wet or too dry - its protective barrier struggles to hold together.

02 · Around Your Home

It might not be food or soap - it's fabric

Tap each card to watch what's really happening, and what it does to skin.

Bedsheets Tap to reveal
Dust Mites & Sweat

Millions of dust mites feed on shed skin in sheets. Overnight sweat and body heat let bacteria multiply fast against skin that's already relaxed and warm.

Pillow Covers Tap to reveal
Closest Contact Zone

A 2-year-old pillow can be up to 10% dust-mite waste by weight - pressed against your face and cheeks for 7-plus hours nightly.

Towels Tap to reveal
Damp & Reused

Left slightly wet on a hook between uses, towels become a warm breeding ground for fungi and bacteria - rubbed directly onto already-vulnerable skin.

Washer Drum Tap to reveal
Leftover Residue

Detergent and softener film can stick to the drum and fabric, especially with overloading, cold quick cycles, or skipped extra rinses.

03 · Rarely Talked About

Six more triggers hiding in plain sight

These rarely get blamed - because they don't look like "skincare" at all.

Hard Water Tap to reveal
Mineral Buildup

Calcium and magnesium in hard water react with soap, leaving scummy residue on skin that strips natural oils and disrupts the barrier - worse for eczema-prone skin.

Nickel & Metal Tap to reveal
The #1 Contact Allergen

Coins, zips, bra hooks, spectacle frames, keys, and phone cases all release nickel. It's the single most common cause of allergic contact dermatitis worldwide.

AC & Dry Air Tap to reveal
Moisture Pulled Out

Constant air-conditioning lowers indoor humidity, speeding up trans-epidermal water loss. Skin gets tight, flaky, and more reactive - the opposite problem to outdoor humidity.

Brand-New Clothes Tap to reveal
That "New Smell" Is a Warning

Unwashed new fabric can carry formaldehyde finishing resins and azo dyes. The chemical smell you notice is often the finishing agent releasing into the air and onto skin.

Leather Straps & Shoes Tap to reveal
Chromium From Tanning

Chrome-tanned leather in shoes and watch straps can release chromium salts, especially in warm, sweaty contact zones - a well-known cause of "shoe dermatitis."

Thermal Receipts Tap to reveal
BPA on Your Fingertips

Thermal paper is coated with free BPA or BPS. It transfers to skin on contact - and absorbs faster if hands have hand sanitiser or lotion on them first.

06 · The Real Chemistry

Two different reactions, two different molecules

"Sensitive skin" isn't one thing chemically. It's usually one of two distinct pathways - and knowing which helps explain why it keeps coming back.

Irritant Contact Dermatitis
Minutes–hours · no immune memory needed
  1. Surfactants like sodium lauryl sulfate (common in detergents & soaps) wedge themselves into the skin's lipid bilayer.
  2. They dissolve ceramides, cholesterol & free fatty acids - the "mortar" that cements skin cells together.
  3. With the mortar gone, trans-epidermal water loss (TEWL) rises sharply and the barrier becomes porous.
  4. Skin's natural surface pH - usually a protective 4.7–5.5 "acid mantle" - drifts upward, disabling the enzymes that rebuild the barrier.
  5. Nerve endings sit closer to the surface and fire directly → stinging, dryness, and redness, even on a first-ever exposure.
Allergic Contact Dermatitis
24–72 hrs · needs prior sensitization
  1. Tiny reactive molecules called haptens - nickel ions (Ni²⁺), chromium salts, formaldehyde resin, azo-dye breakdown products - are too small to be "seen" by the immune system alone.
  2. Once the barrier is weakened, haptens penetrate and bind covalently to skin proteins, forming a hapten-protein complex the body now reads as foreign.
  3. Langerhans cells (dendritic immune cells in the epidermis) capture this complex and travel to nearby lymph nodes.
  4. There, they "train" T-cells to recognise it - a silent first round called sensitization, with no visible reaction yet.
  5. On the next exposure, memory T-cells recognise the same complex and release cytokines (IFN-γ, TNF-α), activating keratinocytes - a delayed Type IV reaction that peaks a day or two later.

A healthy stratum corneum sits around pH 4.7–5.5 - acidic enough to keep barrier-building enzymes active and unfriendly to many bacteria. Alkaline surfactants and hard-water minerals can push that number up within a single wash, and it takes hours for skin to buffer itself back down.

07 · Simple Fixes

Small habits, calmer skin

🔥
Hot-water wash weekly
Sheets, pillowcases & towels in hot water kills dust mites and clears residue.
🌬️
Pat dry, don't rub
Especially underarms, toes, and skin folds - humidity loves trapped moisture.
🧴
Fragrance-free detergent
Add an extra rinse cycle to clear leftover surfactant from fabric.
🧽
Clean the washer drum
Run an empty hot cycle monthly to clear soap film and mould.
🩸
Choose low-nickel metal
Surgical steel or titanium for jewellery, watches and spectacle frames.
💦
Soften hard water
A shower filter or softener reduces mineral buildup on skin.
🌀
Use a humidifier near AC
Counters the moisture loss from continuous air-conditioning.
👕
Wash new clothes first
One wash clears most residual dyes and finishing chemicals before wearing.
