🩸 What HbA1c actually measures

HbA1c reflects how much glucose has quietly attached itself to hemoglobin inside red blood cells, over roughly the last 8–12 weeks (a red blood cell's lifespan). This plan is built around that exact window.

Hemoglobin (Hb) + Glucose → (slow, non-enzymatic glycation) HbA1c
Rate of glycation ∝ average blood glucose over ~90–120 days
↓ average daily glucose swings → ↓ HbA1c over the next 8–12 weeks

Which is why every recipe below is chosen for one job: keeping the glucose curve after a meal low and slow, meal after meal, for twelve weeks straight.

🌿 The five levers this plan pulls

🌾 Low-GI grains

Ragi, barnyard millet, red and brown rice replace polished white rice. Their fibre and resistant starch slow starch breakdown, so glucose enters the blood gradually instead of in a spike.

🫘 Fibre & pulses

Moong, methi and vegetables add soluble fibre that forms a gel in the gut, physically slowing how fast sugar from the same meal can be absorbed.

🐟 Lean protein

Fish and chicken breast barely move blood glucose at all, and they keep Mr. Vittal full longer, which means fewer snack-driven spikes between meals.

🌶️ Functional spices

Turmeric, curry leaf, cinnamon and fenugreek are used at real, everyday-cooking doses. They're a supporting cast, not a substitute for the medicine or the meal plan.

🫗 Portion & oil control

Oil is measured in teaspoons, not ladles, and carbohydrate portions are fixed per plate, so no single ingredient (however healthy) is allowed to overload a meal.

⚖️ How every plate is balanced

Every lunch and dinner in this plan follows the same simple ratio, so nothing is ever too much or too little:

Plate = ½ non-starchy vegetable + ¼ low-GI grain/millet + ¼ protein (dal, fish, or chicken)
+ 1 tsp oil/ghee for cooking + spices to taste → balanced glycemic load per meal

🧂 Kitchen wisdom for every day

🛢️ Oil

Max 3–4 teaspoons of gingelly (sesame) oil or ghee per person per day, measured, not poured. Fat helps absorb turmeric's curcumin but excess fat slows digestion and blunts the benefit.

⏱️ Timing

Three fixed meals, one small evening snack, no meal skipped. Skipping meals often causes a bigger glucose swing at the next one.

💧 Hydration

2.5–3 litres of water a day, buttermilk counted separately. Well hydrated kidneys handle glucose excretion more efficiently.

🍚 Rice portion

One level cup of cooked rice/millet per main meal, always paired with dal or vegetable, never eaten alone.

🚶 Movement

A 15–20 minute walk within 30 minutes of the largest meal measurably blunts the after-meal glucose spike.

🩺 Monitoring

Fasting glucose weekly, HbA1c at week 12. Any medication changes go through the doctor, not this plan.
